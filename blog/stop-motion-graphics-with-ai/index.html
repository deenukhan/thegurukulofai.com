How to Make Stop-Motion Graphic Videos with AI

Production-grade stop-motion graphics, made with AI. The full process end to end: what the style is, how the automation works, and how you make finished videos by doing almost nothing.

By Deenu Khan · 26 June 2026 · 8 min read

You have probably seen these videos. A flat coloured screen sits empty for a beat, and then cut-out elements come flying in from the edges and lock together into a sharp, witty scene. It looks like a designed piece of motion graphics, the kind a studio would charge a fortune for, and yet it moves with a rhythm that feels alive.

Here is the promise, and it is a big one. You can produce these at studio quality with AI, and the numbers are not small:

  • Around 20 times cheaper than commissioning motion graphics the traditional way.
  • About 100 times faster. Minutes and hours, not weeks.
  • Almost fully automated. An AI assistant does the heavy lifting, and your job is mostly to point and approve.

Here is the chess scene from the cover actually moving, so you can see exactly what we are building towards:

By the end of this post you will understand the whole process, and you will have the exact tools to do it yourself.

What you are making

Before the how, it helps to name the style, because once you can name its parts you can recreate it on purpose. This is a premium editorial motion-graphics look, and it comes from a few specific ingredients working together:

  • Halftone elements. The black-and-white pieces carry a visible dot pattern, the kind you see in printed magazines. This is what gives them that crafted, editorial texture instead of a flat digital feel.
  • Clean cut edges. Every element has a crisp outline and a soft shadow, so it reads as a distinct object sitting on top of the scene.
  • Flat, bold colour. The background is one strong, even colour with no shading, which makes everything pop.
  • A sharp idea. Each scene carries a small concept or visual pun rather than just being decorative. The idea is what makes it memorable.

Then there is the motion, which is the part that turns a still into a scene. The frame does not simply wobble or drift. It assembles itself. It opens on an empty background and builds, piece by piece, until the full composition is in place. That building motion is the signature of the whole style.

The big idea: you direct, the AI builds

The reason this is so fast and cheap is that you do not do the fiddly work yourself. The whole process runs inside Claude Code, the AI assistant that acts as your director. You set Claude Code up once to handle two jobs for you:

  1. Study references and generate your own version, shaped to your brand, so the output looks like yours and never like a copy.
  2. Turn those still images into finished stop-motion videos.

In practice these become two small, repeatable automations that the assistant runs on command. The result is a workflow where you only ever do two things:

  • Paste in some references.
  • Say which generated images you like.

Claude Code takes care of everything in between: the analysis, the brand-matching, the image generation, and the conversion into video. Your attention goes where it actually matters, into taste and direction, instead of into busywork.

One important note on the other tools: do not lock yourself into a single image or video product. Claude Code is the director that ties it all together, but the actual image and video generation can run on whichever platform you like. Pick the ones whose results you prefer and keep the process the same.

Set up once

There are only two things to set up in Claude Code, and you do them a single time.

1. Connect Claude Code to your image tool. Claude Code can reach out and operate other services through a standard connection called an MCP. Think of it as a clean, secure way for Claude Code to use another app on your behalf. You connect it to an image platform once, and from then on it can generate images for you on its own. You can use whichever platform you prefer:

  • Magnific
  • Higgsfield
  • Runway ML
  • ImagineArt

They all work well, so choose the one whose images and interface suit you.

2. Add the two skills to Claude Code. These are the automations that do the real work, one for analysing references and generating on-brand stills, and one for turning those stills into motion. You can download both further down this page and drop them straight into Claude Code.

Set this up once, and after that, making a video is just a short conversation with Claude Code.

The clever bit, in plain words

It is worth understanding why these videos build themselves, because it is exactly what separates them from ordinary AI video.

  • Most AI video takes a finished picture and adds a gentle wobble or a slow zoom. Nothing is really happening.
  • This approach starts from an empty version of the scene, just the bare background, and ends on the full image.

Now the tool has a real job, because the only honest way to travel from an empty surface to a finished one is to bring the pieces in. So it slides them from the edges and snaps them into place, and the scene assembles. You do not engineer this yourself, the motion step handles it, but knowing the idea helps you understand what you are looking at, and why it looks so much more deliberate than a typical AI clip.

Make it yours, then scale

The part that keeps your work original is the analysis combined with your brand instructions. You are not copying a reference, you are learning its recipe and re-cooking it in your own colours, your own subjects, and your own ideas. That is why the output looks like you and not like the thing you started from.

And once a single video works, the same flow makes the next, and the one after that:

  • Lock your look once. Colours, element style, and the kind of ideas you tell.
  • Vary only the concept for each new piece.
  • Batch them. A week of content can come out of one afternoon.

That is what scaling really means here. Same system, endless output.
