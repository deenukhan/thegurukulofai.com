A product shoot in India can cost ₹40,000 to ₹2,00,000. You book a studio. You hire a photographer, a model, a stylist. You wait two weeks for edits.

AI changes the maths. One person can now produce a full campaign in a day. Packshots, hero shots, lifestyle scenes, editorial frames, even video — all from a single product photo.

This is the exact workflow I use. Follow it top to bottom and you will have a complete set of brand visuals by the end.

## What you can actually make

Before the how, look at the range. Every image below started as one flat product photo.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/category-examples.webp", "A grid of AI product photography across categories", "One workflow, many categories — beauty, fashion, fragrance, accessories." %}

The point is not a single pretty picture. It is a *system* that holds your product consistent across dozens of shots. That consistency is what makes a brand look real.

## The toolkit

You need three things:

- **An image model** for the shots — Nano Banana, Seedream, or Higgsfield.
- **A consistency method** so the product stays identical across every frame.
- **A video model** to turn the best stills into motion — Kling or Veo.

That is it. No studio. No team. The skill is in the prompting and the direction, not the budget.

## Step 1 — Lock the packshot

Start clean. A packshot is your product on a plain, controlled background. It becomes the reference for everything else.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/packshot-pelexia.webp", "Clean studio packshot of a product", "The packshot is your source of truth. Get it sharp and well-lit first." %}

Keep the prompt boring on purpose. Describe the product, a seamless background, soft studio light, and a clear camera angle. Resist adding drama here. Drama comes later.

## Step 2 — Build the hero shot

Now you add mood. The hero shot is the image that stops the scroll — the one for your homepage banner or your launch post.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/luxury-perfume-bottle.webp", "Luxury perfume bottle hero shot with dramatic lighting", "Same product, new world. Light and setting do the heavy lifting." %}

Push the concept further when the product allows it. A fragrance can live underwater. A serum can sit in a beam of morning light. The product stays exact; the world around it changes.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/underwater-perfume.webp", "Perfume bottle photographed underwater with bubbles", "A concept shot like this would cost lakhs to produce for real." %}

For small products, get in close. Macro shots sell texture — the drop on a dropper, the sheen on a cap.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/serum-dropper.webp", "Macro shot of a serum dropper with a single drop", "Macro detail builds trust. People buy what they can almost touch." %}

## Step 3 — Put it on a model

Lifestyle shots are where conversions happen. People want to see the product used by someone like them.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/lifestyle-model.webp", "Model using a beauty product in a lifestyle setting", "Lifestyle frames give context — who it is for and when they use it." %}

This is the hardest step to keep consistent. Your model must look the same across shots, and your product must not warp. Use a fixed character reference and a tight product reference together. Generate in small batches and reject anything that drifts.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/beauty-campaign-lipstick.webp", "Beauty campaign close-up of lipstick on a model", "Close beauty frames work well for ads — bold, simple, product-forward." %}

## Step 4 — Shoot the editorial campaign

This is the level that makes people ask which agency you used. Editorial frames are about styling and attitude, not just the product.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/editorial-jewellery.webp", "Editorial jewellery photograph with strong styling", "Editorial jewellery — the styling carries as much weight as the piece." %}

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/fashion-handbag-editorial.webp", "High-fashion editorial shot featuring a handbag", "Fashion accessories shine in editorial setups with confident composition." %}

For fashion and apparel, think in campaigns, not single images. A runway look, a street scene, an action frame — together they read like a real brand drop.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/fashion-runway-look.webp", "Runway fashion look", "Build a set, not a single image — that is what reads as a real campaign." %}

The same logic scales to any category. Lifestyle and outdoor brands can stage shots that would need a full crew on location.

{% figure "https://tgai-cdn.b-cdn.net/blog/ai-product-photography-d2c/surf-action-campaign.webp", "Action sports campaign shot near the ocean", "Location, action, weather — all generated, no crew, no travel." %}

## Step 5 — Turn the best stills into video

A still gets a like. A video gets saved and shared. Take your strongest hero shot and animate it.

{% bunnyVideo "db7f486e-066c-47ed-bd24-b59a06010d15", "A still product shot brought to life as a short video." %}

Feed the image into a video model with a short, specific motion prompt — a slow push-in, a gentle rotation, light drifting across the surface. Keep it under five seconds. Subtle motion looks premium; busy motion looks fake.

## The mistakes to avoid

- **Skipping the packshot.** Without a clean reference, your product drifts in every shot.
- **Changing the product to fit the scene.** Change the world, never the product.
- **Generating one at a time.** Work in batches, then cut hard. Reject 80%.
- **Over-animating video.** Less motion, more polish.
- **No consistency method.** This is the line between "AI slop" and a real brand.

## Put it together

One product. A packshot, three hero shots, two lifestyle frames, an editorial set, and a video. That is a full campaign — the kind that used to need a studio, a crew, and two weeks.
